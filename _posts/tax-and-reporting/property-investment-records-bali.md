A company signs a lease for a property in Bali and pays several months up front. Before the first tenant moves in, the directors also pay for air conditioning, repairs and a security deposit out of three different bank accounts. By the time the property earns rent, the owners want to know how much they invested, what the tenant owes and when the business can repay some of the money they advanced. Answering those questions takes a record of each agreement as well as the bank movements.

This guide follows a property held to earn rent. It covers the records to keep from the first acquisition or lease payment through operations, financing and handover. The examples are in Indonesian rupiah and use accrual records to explain when rent was earned and when costs arose. Confirm the accounting basis and tax treatment for your entity and the signed arrangement. If your operation provides short stays through booking platforms, use a separate booking register to tie those reservations and deductions to settlements. ## Define which business is on each agreement

Make a register with one entry per property and a consistent property code. Record the name shown on the acquisition or head lease paperwork, the party that signs the tenant agreement, the name on recurring supplier contracts, and the bank accounts used to collect or pay. Save the signed contracts together with amendments, receipts and the dates when access or control changes. A property code is useful for analysing results, while the legal entity determines which set of books you are keeping.

Add the cash obligations to the same register. For a lease, record the commencement and end dates, options, rent increases, deposit return terms and who bears repairs, insurance and renewal costs. Make a payment schedule with both the due date and the period it covers. If you collect a maintenance contribution or charge utilities under a tenant agreement, record each component and whether the charge is fixed or reconciled to actual costs. A reminder on a calendar can then point to the agreement and amount behind it.

If you assemble the register after operations have begun, start by reconstructing the balances still open at your chosen cut-off date. Check the next head lease payment against the signed schedule. Compare refundable deposits paid and received with the receipts and any subsequent settlements. Ask the prior manager where unpaid tenant invoices and unbilled costs were tracked. Keep a dated record of what the new bookkeeper could verify and who will resolve each outstanding difference. This opening check prevents an old deposit or cost from dropping out of the accounts when records move to a new system.

Where the contracting party, bank payer or invoice recipient differs, collect evidence before assigning the movement. For example, a shareholder may pay a water system invoice while a tenant pays rent to the company. Keep the vendor bill, payment proof and the company's approval for how the funding will be treated. A signed lease might also place a service charge with the tenant even when the provider invoices the owner. A brief note about who is responsible, who paid and what is recoverable is often enough to bring the right questions to the accountant. If the arrangement is still being agreed, record the issue and follow it through to resolution.

## Build a schedule for the money spent before rental income begins

Group the initial payments by purpose. A refundable deposit has return conditions to track. Advance rent relates to the rights and periods in the lease. The consideration paid to acquire a property may need a breakdown between components, and transaction costs can require separate assessment. Equipment and fit-out work have their own supporting invoices and dates when ready for use. A single transfer to a seller, lessor or contractor can contain several components. Ask for the settlement statement and retain the breakdown alongside the bank reference.

Keep a lease payment schedule even if your accountant records the arrangement through a right-of-use asset and lease liability. Show amounts due, amounts paid and months remaining, with the original terms and any changes at renewal. If an advance covers several years, preserve the contractual periods and how the amount is applied. The monthly cash budget still needs the next transfer date even if the accounting entries follow a different pattern. For a property purchased outright, maintain a schedule of property and equipment costs with the supporting agreements and the date each asset or component became available for use. [IAI’s introduction to the Indonesian leases standard](https://web.iaiglobal.or.id/PSAK-Umum/84) · [IFRS 16 overview of leases](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ifrs-16-leases/)

Ask your accountant to document the classification under the applicable reporting framework. Under PSAK 240, Indonesia’s full standard on investment property, a property held for rent or capital appreciation can be accounted for differently from premises used by the entity in its operations. Mixed use, significant services provided to occupants and changes in the way the building is used need review. Record how the property is actually used, including the dates and scope of any change. Where the owner begins running part of the building as its own office, for example, save a floor plan or other evidence of how the space is divided. [IAI’s summary of PSAK 240](https://web.iaiglobal.or.id/PSAK-Umum/15) · [IAI guidance on mixed-use properties and services](https://web.iaiglobal.or.id/assets/materi/Sertifikasi/CA/modul/pk_19/files/basic-html/page121.html)

## Keep the tenant account current

For each tenant, maintain a ledger that shows opening balances, charges by billing period, payments, credits and amounts still outstanding. Include rent, any separately billed utilities or maintenance, and deposits. Match each payment to the tenant and invoice, then trace it to the bank statement. If a tenant pays several months together, record the coverage and what remains unapplied. If someone transfers an amount without a clear reference, ask what it covers and keep the response with the reconciliation. That trail makes the next arrears review easier.

Track refundable tenant deposits in a schedule with the amount collected, any valid deductions, the amount returned and the balance held. While the business owes a cash refund under the agreement, the amount held is a liability. When a tenant moves out, save the handover inspection, the evidence supporting a charge and the settlement calculation. The tenant ledger should explain what was done with the deposit, including any amount applied against rent under the agreement. Agree who can approve deductions or refunds and preserve the transfer receipt with the move-out documents. [IAS 32 guidance on financial liabilities](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ias-32-financial-instruments-presentation/)

Suppose a tenant pays IDR 30 million when moving in on 1 February. In this example, IDR 20 million covers February and March rent at IDR 10 million a month, and IDR 10 million is a refundable security deposit. Assume a simple operating lease with level rent and use the deposit’s face amount. The example excludes tax. At the end of February, IDR 10 million is earned for that month, IDR 10 million still relates to March and IDR 10 million is held as a deposit. In March the remaining IDR 10 million of rent is earned even if no further payment arrives. The deposit stays on the schedule until the agreed conditions for its use or return are met. [IFRS Interpretations Committee explanation of operating lease income](https://www.ifrs.org/news-and-events/updates/ifric/2022/ifric-update-september-2022/)

Now assume the April rent of IDR 10 million is due in full and the tenant pays IDR 8 million. If the remaining IDR 2 million is still owed under the agreement, keep it in receivables and hand it to the person following up. Review arrears by tenant and due date. If collection becomes doubtful or both sides agree to a credit, document the facts for whoever prepares the accounts. This example gives the owner three figures to review at the end of April. They can see the month's rent, cash received and outstanding balance, and they can check the untouched deposit separately.

## Track repairs, improvements and shared costs

Give each invoice a property code, service period and purpose. File work orders, photos or other completion evidence with larger invoices. A routine air-conditioning service and a new installation can appear on the same bill. Ask for enough detail to separate them and review whether an amount is a running expense, a capital improvement or a replacement component. When replacing a major component, check whether part of the original asset is still recorded in the books. The decision should follow the accounting framework and evidence of the work performed. An invoice labelled renovation can cover several different kinds of work. [IAI guidance on servicing and replacement costs](https://web.iaiglobal.or.id/assets/materi/Sertifikasi/CA/modul/pk_19/files/basic-html/page122.html)

Allocate recurring shared costs on a consistent and explainable basis. A security contract covering two properties may be allocated by the hours assigned to each site if the rota supports it. Common-area costs might follow the terms of the lease or the areas served. Keep the calculation, explain material changes and record whether any portion can be recovered from the tenant. This gives the owner a property-level margin without losing the original bill or creating duplicate entries for the business.

Keep two dates for significant jobs when useful. The contractor's expected payment date belongs in the cash forecast. The completion or ready-for-use date helps the accountant review financial reporting. If works were accepted in December but the invoice arrives in January, the December file should contain the commitment and evidence that allows the cost and any liability to be assessed. At handover, record the equipment, warranties and work carried out. Those details matter if the tenant later asks who must maintain it or if a buyer wants to understand what is included.

## Reconcile finance and owner movements separately

Keep a schedule for each shareholder advance, loan or capital contribution. Record who provided the funds, the currency and principal, the legal entity receiving them and the evidence of the arrangement. Track any interest or fees separately and reconcile the remaining loan balance to statements and repayments. When the owner pays a business cost personally, keep both the cost evidence and the record of how it was funded. A later reimbursement should reconcile to the original amount owed. If an amount is formally contributed as capital, retain the corporate records supporting that classification.

Use a monthly report that shows both how the property performed and how its cash moved. The performance view can show earned rent, costs incurred for that period and noncash expenses such as applicable depreciation. The cash movement view tracks rent received, supplier payments, improvements, loan principal repayments and transfers to or from owners. It should also explain the amounts held for deposits and the bills due before the next likely rent receipt. An owner deciding on a distribution or loan repayment needs the bank position and the commitments behind it, plus any approvals or restrictions that apply.

For example, assume a property earns IDR 24 million in June, collects IDR 20 million and incurs IDR 7 million in operating costs, all paid that month. It also pays IDR 9 million to install a new system under a separately tracked improvement project and repays IDR 3 million of loan principal. The June rental margin before other accounting expenses is IDR 17 million. Subtract the three payments from the IDR 20 million collected and June generated IDR 1 million of net cash. The remaining IDR 4 million of rent is an open receivable if the tenant still owes it. Ask the preparer to review how to classify the improvement and when to recognise any related depreciation, and keep both the contractor bill and the financing statement with the close.

## Give your adviser records by activity and location

Preserve the signed agreement, invoice, bank receipt and any withholding evidence for each category of receipts. Indonesia’s final income tax rules for land and building rentals exclude income from lodging and associated accommodation services under Article 2 of Government Regulation 34/2017. Qualifying accommodation services can fall within the regional tax on certain goods and services, known as PBJT. Send your adviser the agreement, the property location, who supplies the services, the billing breakdown and evidence of how the arrangement works in practice. Ask which national and local rules apply to each activity. [Government Regulation 34/2017, Article 2](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Download/54909/PP%20Nomor%2034%20Tahun%202017.pdf) · [Law 1/2022, Articles 50 and 53](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Download/189520/UU%20Nomor%201%20Tahun%202022.pdf)

Where a payer withholds tax, retain the withholding evidence and tie it to gross rent, the amount withheld and the net funds received. Keep the payment receipt where responsibility for payment falls to the recipient. If payment arrives short and the reason is unclear, keep the difference open while you ask the payer. Give your adviser a month-by-month schedule of contracted rent, amounts billed, amounts received and charges such as maintenance, security or facilities. The rental tax rules can include related charges in gross rental value even when billed under separate agreements. If the same business also runs a serviced accommodation unit or has properties in different regencies, keep separate records for each location and activity. [Government Regulation 34/2017, Articles 3 and 4](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Download/54909/PP%20Nomor%2034%20Tahun%202017.pdf)

## Prepare a handover file before the property changes hands

If you sell the investment, assign a lease or hand the property back at expiry, agree which date and documents establish the cut-off. Reconcile what the parties owe each other, which rents have been collected for periods after that date, and where each tenant's deposit goes. Get written evidence when a deposit or other balance transfers to a successor. Keep invoices for completion costs, loan closing statements, receipts for funds received, and any amounts still subject to conditions. Give the accountant the full settlement workings, even if one net payment appears on the bank statement.

Keep an indexed file with the acquisition or lease evidence, spending and improvement schedules, monthly rent roll, bank reconciliations, owner and loan schedules, and the last agreed balances for tenants and service providers. The rent roll should show each tenant and the rental period, agreed charges, payments and arrears. Check that the closing cash and balances tie to the ledger before a new manager, buyer or accountant takes over. If a tenant disputes repairs or a lender keeps back a balance pending a final document, put the amount, owner and expected resolution date on a handover list.

Start with the signed paperwork, the most recent month of bank transactions and the rent and deposit schedules. Select one property and trace a rent payment, an improvement and an owner movement from the bank to the supporting document and through the books. Use the gaps you find to set a routine for your next close. A good first discussion with a bookkeeper covers how many properties and entities are involved, what evidence you already have and who currently keeps track of tenant balances.
