Your Indonesian company hires an overseas consultant. The invoice is ready to pay, but accounts payable still needs to confirm how much to send and whether it must withhold Indonesian tax. Start with the contract and the consultant’s tax status, ideally before the fee comes due.

**PPh Pasal 26** (*Pajak Penghasilan Pasal 26*, or Article 26 income tax) covers Indonesian withholding tax on specified Indonesian-source income paid or payable to non-residents. This guide focuses on payments made by Indonesian businesses, including foreign-investment companies known as PT PMA, and explains what to check before a payment, when a treaty matters and what to keep for monthly reporting. [Indonesian Income Tax Law, Article 26](https://www.pajak.go.id/sites/default/files/2023-05/SDSN_2023_3.0.pdf.pdf)

## When should an Indonesian company check PPh 26?

Review overseas consulting and other professional service fees, software or brand licence payments, and relevant interest or dividends. Article 26(1) generally makes the Indonesian payer responsible for withholding on covered income paid or payable to a non-resident. The treatment can differ where the payment is connected to an Indonesian permanent establishment, a taxable business presence called a *bentuk usaha tetap* (BUT). Specified payments to Indonesian residents or permanent establishments can fall under PPh 23. [Indonesian Income Tax Law, Articles 2, 23 and 26](https://www.pajak.go.id/sites/default/files/2023-05/SDSN_2023_3.0.pdf.pdf)

## What to check before an overseas payment

1. **Confirm who earns the income.** Record the contracting party, its tax residence and applicable tax identification details. Ask about any office, personnel or other business activity in Indonesia that needs review.
2. **Read the contract and invoice together.** Identify what the company is paying for, the source of the income and any separately priced services or licence rights. For services, document where the work is performed and whether anyone comes to Indonesia. Check when the fee is paid, made available or becomes due, and whether the supplier agreed a gross fee or a net amount after withholding.
3. **Check domestic treatment and any treaty.** Determine the applicable classification under Indonesian law. If the recipient claims treaty treatment, check the relevant article and confirm eligibility and documents before applying it.

Review the transaction before the first relevant payment event. Under Article 26(1), making the income available or reaching the due date can trigger withholding even if accounts payable has yet to make the bank transfer. [Indonesian Income Tax Law, Article 26](https://www.pajak.go.id/sites/default/files/2023-05/SDSN_2023_3.0.pdf.pdf) · [DJP guidance on Article 26 timing, slides 115–116](https://www.pajak.go.id/sites/default/files/2026-09/Materi%20Aspek%20Perpajakan%20Parpol%2021092026.pdf)

## When can a tax treaty change the withholding?

Indonesia calls its double tax agreements *Persetujuan Penghindaran Pajak Berganda* (P3B). An applicable treaty can reduce Indonesian withholding or limit Indonesia’s right to tax particular income. Check the treaty article for the income type and whether the recipient meets the conditions, including beneficial ownership where relevant. The Ministry of Finance generally requires the overseas recipient to provide a completed Form DGT for the relevant period. A qualifying certificate of residence can replace official certification of the form in the ordinary Form DGT procedure. The payer checks the evidence and applies domestic rules where the treaty requirements are unmet. [DJP treaty texts and instruments](https://www.pajak.go.id/taxtreaty-mli) · [Ministry of Finance Regulation PMK 112/2025, Articles 2 and 8–10](https://jdih.kemenkeu.go.id/api/download/8830a44a-12f5-4717-84c0-fed0e6239bdf/2025pmkeuangan112.pdf)

## What to calculate, pay and report each month

For the relevant tax period, calculate the withholding where due, record the gross amount and tax withheld, and reconcile those figures with the net amount paid to the supplier. Prepare the withholding evidence, remit any tax due and file the applicable return with Indonesia’s Directorate General of Taxes (DJP). Keep the contract, invoice, calculation, supplier details, treaty documents, payment proof and filing acknowledgement together. [Indonesian Income Tax Law, Article 26](https://www.pajak.go.id/sites/default/files/2023-05/SDSN_2023_3.0.pdf.pdf) · [PMK 112/2025, Articles 14 and 16](https://jdih.kemenkeu.go.id/api/download/8830a44a-12f5-4717-84c0-fed0e6239bdf/2025pmkeuangan112.pdf)

Covered payments to overseas companies are generally reported through the unified monthly income tax return (*SPT Masa PPh Unifikasi*). Article 26 withholding on an individual’s employment, services or activities uses the separate PPh 21/26 monthly return. Where treaty treatment eliminates Indonesian withholding, PMK 112/2025 still requires reporting and withholding evidence for income covered by that rule. [DJP reporting regulation PER-11/PJ/2025, Articles 3 and 14–15](https://www.pajak.go.id/sites/default/files/lampiran/PER-11_PJ_2025_0.pdf) · [DJP amendment PER-12/PJ/2026](https://pajak.go.id/id/peraturan/perubahan-atas-peraturan-direktur-jenderal-pajak-nomor-11pj2025-tentang-ketentuan) · [PMK 112/2025, Article 14](https://jdih.kemenkeu.go.id/api/download/8830a44a-12f5-4717-84c0-fed0e6239bdf/2025pmkeuangan112.pdf)

Under the ordinary deadlines, remit any PPh 26 due by the 15th of the following month and file the applicable return within 20 days after the tax period ends. Where the deadline falls on a holiday covered by the rules, it moves to the next working day. [PMK 81/2024 as amended, Articles 94, 100, 171 and 173](https://jdih.kemenkeu.go.id/dok/pmk-81-tahun-2024)

## Common mistakes with overseas payments

- Using the payee’s bank country as evidence of tax residence.
- Reviewing withholding after a fee has already been paid or fallen due.
- Giving a bundled service and software invoice one classification without reading the licence terms.
- Applying treaty relief before checking the Form DGT, its coverage period and the treaty conditions.
- Keeping the bank transfer receipt while losing the contract, review notes or filing evidence.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is every international payment subject to PPh 26?

Review the nature and source of the income and the recipient’s tax status. A transfer abroad can cover very different transactions, from a royalty to a payment for services. The bank destination alone tells you little about which rule applies. Check the relevant treaty conditions if the recipient claims relief. [Indonesian Income Tax Law, Article 26](https://www.pajak.go.id/sites/default/files/2023-05/SDSN_2023_3.0.pdf.pdf)

### Can PPh 26 apply to consulting delivered remotely?

It can be relevant. Review the domestic treatment of the service fee, the recipient’s tax residence and business presence, and the treaty provisions that apply to that type of income. Keep evidence of where and how the work was done, including any work performed during visits to Indonesia. [Indonesian Income Tax Law, Article 26](https://www.pajak.go.id/sites/default/files/2023-05/SDSN_2023_3.0.pdf.pdf) · [DJP treaty texts and instruments](https://www.pajak.go.id/taxtreaty-mli)

### What documents does a foreign company need to claim treaty treatment?

For a typical supplier, ask for the completed Form DGT covering the relevant period. If the supplier uses a certificate of residence to meet the official certification requirement, check that the certificate qualifies. Keep the contract and invoice alongside the form, any residence certificate and the relevant submission receipt. [PMK 112/2025, Articles 8–10 and 16](https://jdih.kemenkeu.go.id/api/download/8830a44a-12f5-4717-84c0-fed0e6239bdf/2025pmkeuangan112.pdf)

### Do I still have to report the payment if a treaty eliminates withholding?

Yes, for income covered by the treaty reporting rule. The Indonesian payer still needs to prepare evidence of withholding treatment and report the transaction in the applicable return. Save the treaty documents and filing acknowledgement so you can explain why no tax was remitted. [PMK 112/2025, Article 14](https://jdih.kemenkeu.go.id/api/download/8830a44a-12f5-4717-84c0-fed0e6239bdf/2025pmkeuangan112.pdf)
