A useful tax calendar records both the verified filing or payment date and the internal work needed before it. Start by confirming which obligations apply to the company, then work backwards to document collection, reconciliation, review and approval.

This guide provides an operational calendar template. It does not give a single statutory schedule for all Indonesian companies: the applicable tax types, period, company status and current rules need to be checked first.

## Build a register of applicable obligations

Ask the person responsible for tax reporting to identify each obligation that applies to the company. Give each one its own row, even when several happen in the same month. Keep payment and filing milestones separate so that one is not mistaken for completion of the other.

Use these columns in a spreadsheet or shared task list:

| Column | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Obligation and period | The exact report or payment, and the period it relates to |
| Applicability | Why it applies to this business and who confirmed it |
| Official due date | The verified date for this obligation and period |
| Source and checked date | The official guidance used and when it was checked |
| Documents ready by | Your internal collection deadline |
| Review and approval | The responsible people and internal review dates |
| Payment and submission status | Separate progress fields and references |
| Evidence | A link to the final working file and acknowledgement |

DJP’s [official Coretax resource page](https://www.pajak.go.id/id/reformdjp/coretax) links to guidance for registration, payments and tax reporting. It also notes that guidance can change. Use the relevant current source for the obligation you are recording, rather than treating an old screenshot or a calendar copied from another company as confirmation.

## Work backwards from the verified date

Here is an example of an **internal workflow**, expressed relative to the verified deadline. These intervals are planning choices, not statutory dates:

1. **Ten working days before:** collect the source records and list missing information.
2. **Seven working days before:** reconcile the working figures and resolve questions with the business.
3. **Five working days before:** prepare the draft for review.
4. **Three working days before:** obtain the required approval and confirm the next action.
5. **Before the verified deadline:** complete the agreed payment or filing and retain the evidence.
6. **After submission:** check the acknowledgement and record any follow-up required.

Choose earlier internal dates where the business has complex transactions, several approvers or a history of missing documents. If the verified official date changes, move the dependent tasks too.

## Review the calendar when the business changes

A calendar needs more than a yearly refresh. When the company changes its activities, staffing, registration status or reporting arrangements, ask whether its obligations and working process need review. Record the conclusion, the source and the person who confirmed it.

Keep a visible exceptions list for unresolved items. A row marked “waiting for information” should name the missing document, the person responsible and the next follow-up date.

## Make completion visible

Do not close a task simply because the preparer has finished the draft. Store approval, payment and submission evidence where the next person can find it, and distinguish completed tasks from those awaiting acknowledgement or correction.
