During its first month of trading, a foreign-owned company in Bali might receive a customer deposit, a transfer from a shareholder and a refund from a supplier. Each appears as money coming in. To close the month, the finance team needs to know what each payment relates to, which company it belongs to and how it affects the ledger. Months later, a director should be able to answer those questions without digging through a chat history.

This checklist is for the team closing the books of a PT PMA, an Indonesian limited liability company with foreign investment. It covers the operating records, shareholder funding and cross-border balances that often need attention before the numbers go to directors, tax advisers or the overseas parent. If you are setting up the company's reporting for the first time, start with the [guide to PT PMA accounting and reporting in Bali](/blog/foundations/pt-pma-accounting-services-bali).

## Set the cut-off and assign each handover

Agree the reporting period and work back from the earliest review or filing deadline that depends on the figures. A director abroad might want the management accounts on the eighth working day. Your local tax preparer may need the supplier invoices earlier to review withholding. Put both handovers on the same calendar and give each one an owner. Treat the delivery dates as internal targets and track the applicable filing dates separately.

Maintain a register of the places where the company handles money. Include each bank account and currency, credit card, cash float, payment processor and marketplace account. For each one, record who can export its statements, which legal entity holds it and when the full month can be downloaded. Ask for the opening and closing balance and complete transaction history, including for accounts with little activity. A balance of zero after a new account was opened and funded can still conceal transfers or charges during the month.

Give operations a deadline to send signed contracts, sales records, supplier bills, advances, expenses paid by staff and proof that work was accepted. When someone sends a document through chat, store the underlying file with its invoice or payment reference in the company archive. Name the approver for each unusual item before the month gets busy. A bookkeeper can identify a transfer from an overseas director. The person who authorised it should be able to explain the purpose and provide the agreement or approval.

Set the format of the company books separately from any management report for overseas directors. Indonesia's tax bookkeeping rules require corporate taxpayers to keep books and retain them, their supporting documents and underlying electronic records in Indonesia for ten years. The tax books default to Indonesian and rupiah. If you want to keep them in English with rupiah or in English with US dollars, check eligibility and the applicable notification or approval process before switching. Record what the company has submitted or obtained in the standing close instructions so the next preparer can check which rules the company uses. ## Reconcile the bank and cash before reviewing results

Compare each ledger account to its statement. Begin with the previous signed-off balance, match the month's entries and tie the result to the closing statement balance. Mark items that clear shortly after month-end with the later date and reference. For any older difference, record the amount, date, likely cause, evidence needed and person responsible. Review those balances each month so that an item stays visible until somebody clears it.

Match both sides of transfers between the company's accounts. The originating bank may show a debit on the last working day while the receiving bank posts the credit the next day. Capture both bank references and check where the amount sits at the reporting date. When a transfer also changes currency, preserve the amount sent, amount received, exchange rate and any separately identified fee. The transfer can then be followed across two accounts without distorting operating receipts or expenses.

Reconcile any clearing account that sits between a customer payment and a bank deposit. For example, a payment provider shows IDR 9,000,000 of payments, a separately recorded IDR 250,000 refund and IDR 150,000 of processing fees. The bank deposit should be IDR 8,600,000 if everything settles together. Match the settlement report to the deposit, then connect the payments and refund to their own source records. If one component settles the following month, leave the identified balance on the clearing schedule and verify it against the next payout. This example isolates settlement mechanics. Use your actual invoices and tax records to identify the components of each sale.

For petty cash, agree who counts the cash, when the count is made and how differences are approved. Where staff hold advances for purchases or travel, track the cash given, receipts returned, amount unspent and date the advance is due back or due to be cleared. A team member reimbursed before supplying the receipts should still have an open item. The approver needs to see which receipts are missing and whether more money is owed in either direction.

## Review invoices, deposits and commitments around the cut-off

Look closely at invoices, credit notes, deliveries and customer acceptances on either side of month-end. Check when the company earned the income or incurred the cost under its accounting policy. In an accrual-based example, a contractor finishes and hands over an agreed repair on 28 November and invoices IDR 18,000,000 on 3 December. If the company incurred the cost in November and has yet to record it, support the accrual with the contract, acceptance and later invoice. When the invoice is entered, check that the temporary entry is cleared so the same work appears once.

Make a similar pass through money collected or paid in advance. If a client pays IDR 24,000,000 for work scheduled in a later period, keep the signed agreement, due dates and proof of payment together. Carry the amount on the appropriate schedule and review when performance under the agreement supports recognising revenue. If the company prepays a supplier to reserve materials, track when delivery occurs and see how the supplier applies the advance to the final bill. At sign-off, ask which old deposits need follow-up or might be refundable.

Pull separate lists of overdue customer invoices and supplier bills. Review disputes, credit notes and whether an amount scheduled to be paid is actually due. Keep significant equipment and fit-out purchases visible for an accounting review. A supplier invoice may cover an asset, maintenance on an existing item and several locations. Tag each component and save any breakdown before deciding how it enters the accounts. That same detail helps when the project manager later asks for actual costs by site.

## Clear shareholder transactions and reconcile foreign currency

Start with a separate schedule for each shareholder or related company that moves money with the PT PMA. Record the counterparty, currency, opening balance, new movements, and closing balance. Add the agreement or approval reference and identify expected settlement dates. If the same owner has two entities, match balances to the specific entity named in the documents. Ask the other side for its ledger or confirmation when amounts build up or the expected balances disagree.

For a new shareholder transfer, gather the bank advice and the documents that explain whether it relates to an equity contribution, a documented loan or another transaction. Where the documents and payment reference disagree, assign someone to resolve the mismatch before approving the final classification. For loans, maintain a record of principal, repayments and any agreed interest separately. Flag cross-border amounts, charges or terms that need review by the company's tax adviser before payment or reporting. A clear record of the parties, purpose and original currency makes that review easier.

Suppose a shareholder pays IDR 12,000,000 personally to a supplier for a company repair. The company reimburses the shareholder the following month. Collect the supplier invoice, proof that the shareholder paid, and the company's approval. If the company incurred the repair cost and is obliged to reimburse the shareholder, the close should capture the company cost and the amount due to the shareholder. When the company pays in the next period, match the bank transaction to that outstanding balance. This gives directors a record of the expense in the appropriate month and prevents the reimbursement from adding a second repair charge.

Keep the original currency alongside the local ledger amount for foreign cash, invoices and loans. For a company applying Indonesia's PSAK 221 with rupiah as its functional currency, a foreign-currency transaction is initially translated at the spot rate on the transaction date and outstanding monetary items are translated at the closing rate at the reporting date. Confirm the applicable framework and approved rate source with the preparer. Save the rate, effective date and calculation with the journal so the next reviewer can reproduce it. [Indonesian Institute of Accountants summary of PSAK 221](https://web.iaiglobal.or.id/PSAK-Umum/14)

For example, a USD 8,000 loan principal recorded at IDR 15,500 per USD initially sits at IDR 124,000,000. If the principal is unchanged and the closing rate is IDR 15,650 per USD, the rupiah closing balance is IDR 125,200,000. The movement schedule should explain the IDR 1,200,000 increase as an exchange-rate difference. Keep USD 8,000 visible for cash planning, and let the preparer review the reporting treatment of the difference. Use the latest cash forecast to estimate what the company would need to reserve if it plans to repay the loan soon.

## Tie payroll and tax work to the ledger

Before the tax handover, match the approved payroll register to the ledger and payments. Run through joiners, leavers, one-off bonuses, allowances and reimbursements. Trace net pay to bank transfers and tie unsettled deductions and employer contributions to the closing accounts. If a payment is held back while bank details are corrected, give it a clear status so the team can check that the next run clears it once. Share individual payroll files only with the people responsible for preparing or reviewing them.

Review tax-sensitive transactions with whoever decides the company's reporting treatment. Flag the first invoice for a lease, a payment to an overseas supplier, a new service charge from a group company, or a supplier where the invoice and payment amounts differ. For applicable withholding, show the link between the invoice, supplier payment, tax amount and withholding slip. When VAT is applicable, reconcile the relevant tax invoices against the sales and purchase records. Keep a list of cancellations or documents that still need to be corrected and state which tax period each question affects. [DJP Coretax guidance on returns and transaction records](https://pajak.go.id/id/reformdjp/coretax-spt)

Keep distinct status fields for prepared, approved, paid and filed. For an electronic state revenue payment, retain the state revenue receipt, called BPN, with its validated transaction number, NTPN, where applicable. Keep the submitted return, workings and electronic filing acknowledgement, called BPE, in the filing records. Date and index the records against the period, taxpayer and return type. [Ministry of Finance Regulation 81/2024, Article 102](https://jdih.kemenkeu.go.id/dok/pmk-81-tahun-2024) · [DJP rules on filing acknowledgements, Articles 13–14](https://www.pajak.go.id/en/node/119744)

Where a customer has withheld tax and issued a slip, match the slip to the affected invoice or payment and follow up on a wrong taxpayer identifier, amount or period. DJP's Coretax guide explains where taxpayers can retrieve slips issued by counterparties. [Coretax guide to downloading received withholding slips](https://www.pajak.go.id/coretaxpedia/unduh-bukti-potong)

## Prepare the records that will support LKPM

Keep a monthly schedule for investment costs, project locations and other data needed for the company's investment activity reporting. A PT PMA preparing an LKPM, or *Laporan Kegiatan Penanaman Modal*, uses the Online Single Submission system, known as OSS. The monthly ledger provides a source for the project schedule. Reconcile the reportable items to the relevant activity and location before drafting the separate filing. Ask the site lead for the workforce and operating details the form requires. [Investment Ministry and BKPM Regulation 5/2025, Articles 285–286](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/332573/permeninvesbkpm-no-5-tahun-2025)

Consider a company fitting out a workshop in one location and opening a showroom in another. Operations pays one invoice for work at both sites. Ask the vendor for a breakdown or document the basis for splitting it, then link the amounts to the underlying contract and ledger. Flag amounts whose reporting treatment needs confirmation. At the next quarter-end, the person preparing LKPM can see which project each amount relates to, what was captured in earlier submissions and whether the relevant OSS entries need review. Once the company files, retain the submission evidence with the approved schedule. [LKPM preparation checklist](/blog/compliance/lkpm-notes)

## Approve the close pack and record the exceptions

Put the final trial balance, reporting statements and reconciliations in a dated folder. Add schedules for open customer and supplier balances, deposits or advances, shareholder and intercompany accounts, foreign currency and significant asset purchases. Include a versioned summary of any approved journals, with the rationale and the source documents. If a management report is translated for overseas shareholders, label the currency and period clearly and preserve the bridge to the approved local figures.

Give the reviewer a short summary of unusual movements and decisions. For example, if cash increased by IDR 140,000,000 while sales slowed, show whether the increase came from shareholder funding, a large collection from an earlier month or a transfer. If a supplier balance doubled, explain whether a large project invoice arrived or whether payments were delayed. For a final check, pick one material profit and loss line and one significant balance sheet line. Trace each back to its schedule, ledger and source evidence. You should be able to repeat the check with the files in the pack.

If anything significant is still open, identify its effect on the statements, the decision needed and who owns it. The reviewer can then decide whether the month needs to stay open, whether a clearly supported adjustment can be made, or whether a documented issue can carry forward. Save the approved version and keep later changes traceable. If a correction affects a tax return or an OSS report already submitted, tell the person responsible for that filing so they can review which action is required. At the next close, use the approved ending balances as the starting point and revisit every carried-forward item.

If your PT PMA has fallen behind, start with the last period you can reconcile and identify the first account that stopped tying out. Bring that month, the list of payment channels and a short set of open questions to the person helping with the clean-up. That gives them enough information to agree what to recover first and what a sustainable monthly handover should include.
