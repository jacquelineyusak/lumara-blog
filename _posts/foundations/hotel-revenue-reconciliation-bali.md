The restaurant closes its till at 11 pm. At the front desk, a couple asks to put dinner on their room just before the hotel changes its business date. The next morning, the restaurant report and the guest folio both show the charge. The guest will pay when they check out, and the card provider may settle two days later. By month-end, the hotel needs to explain which department earned the revenue, when it was earned, and where the money is.

This is a hotel accounting workflow for boutique hotels, resorts and guesthouses in Bali. It starts with the nightly close and follows the data into the monthly accounts. A guesthouse with a single front desk can use it with a reservation export and a spreadsheet. A resort with several outlets can give the same checks to different teams. In both cases, the aim is to show how the daily activity produces the numbers an owner uses to make decisions.

## Close the hotel’s business date

A night audit is the hotel’s daily review and close of the business date. The business date is the date the hotel’s systems use for operations and reporting. It can roll after midnight. Decide how the restaurant, front desk and other outlets assign late charges to it. Write down when each team closes and who can approve an exception. The point is to keep a late charge attached to the right day and prevent different reports from using inconsistent cutoffs. Hotel systems such as Oracle OPERA Cloud let teams advance the business date while other end-of-day procedures complete the postings, reports and exports for the date being closed. [Oracle’s end-of-day overview](https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/hospitality/opera-cloud/25.5/ocsuh/c_endofday_procedures.htm)

Have the duty manager review arrivals, guests still in-house and departures before the close. Resolve a guest still marked as due out who extended their stay. Check whether a guest who missed check-in should be marked as a no-show and whether the reservation terms support a fee. Compare active rooms and rates to the room charge report. A complimentary room, an authorised upgrade and a missed automatic charge can have the same effect on a daily total. Each has different evidence and a different next step.

Use the property management system, or PMS, to pull guest folios. A folio is the guest account that shows charges, credits and payments. Save the room and package postings, revenue totals by transaction code, payment totals by tender, and the list of adjustments and errors. If the finance export can run more than once, record the export date and batch reference. A repeat import can duplicate an entire day's sales. Assign someone to check the file landed once and that its totals agree to the original close report. [Oracle’s end-of-day report descriptions](https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/hospitality/opera-cloud/25.2/ocsuh/c_reports_end_of_day.htm)

A short handover gives the morning team something specific to investigate. Record the business date, what was checked, the amount in question, the related folio or receipt, and who will clear the item. If the restaurant interface failed, preserve the outlet report and list the charges staff entered manually. Check that the interface will not post them again when it reconnects. The person who approves a correction should be able to see the original entry and the reason for changing it.

## Count each sale in the department that earned it

Map the PMS transaction codes and point-of-sale, or POS, codes to the accounts that finance reviews. A revenue code for accommodation should feed the rooms accounts. A dinner check belongs with food or beverage sales even if a guest settles it at the front desk. A room charge entry from the restaurant connects the sale to a guest’s account for collection. Keep the outlet sale and the guest balance linked through the check reference so a duplicate entry cannot inflate revenue. Document which system supplies each revenue code to the accounts.

Test a bill paid partly at the restaurant and partly through the front desk. If a guest pays IDR 300,000 by card and charges the remaining IDR 200,000 to their room, the restaurant records its full IDR 500,000 sale. Reconcile the IDR 300,000 to the card terminal totals and IDR 200,000 to the guest folio. Compare the outlet check reference and the destination folio. This also shows whether the system correctly routes split payments. [Oracle Simphony’s room charge integration guide](https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/food-beverage/simphony/simcg/c_opera_connection.htm)

Check the difference with an example. On one business date, the hotel earns IDR 22,500,000 in room revenue, IDR 6,000,000 in restaurant revenue and IDR 1,200,000 in spa revenue. Of the restaurant total, guests pay IDR 4,400,000 at the outlet and move IDR 1,600,000 onto room folios. Total earned revenue for the day is IDR 29,700,000. The IDR 1,600,000 already belongs to restaurant revenue and now sits in the guest balances until collected. If finance imports the POS revenue and then records that folio charge as additional revenue, the daily total would rise to IDR 31,300,000. This example leaves out tax, fees and refunds so the transfer between systems is visible.

Use a similar map for packages. A room sold with a meal, treatment and transfer can arrive as a single reservation price. Agree how the applicable accounting policy will treat the components and check that the system applies the policy consistently. Keep the booking terms, rate plan and actual inclusions with the reconciliation. If a treatment is included, ask how staff record its redemption so it can be tracked without a second sale. If the guest orders an additional treatment at a separate price, capture the new charge and identify who supplied it. If the hotel reports to STR, check which non-room package components its benchmarking rules exclude from room revenue. Review the records used for financial and tax reporting under their applicable rules. [CoStar’s STR historical benchmarking guidelines](https://www.costar.com/products/str-benchmark/resources/guidelines/historical-benchmarking-data-reporting-guidelines)

Review tax and service charge codes on their own lines. Indonesia’s national framework covers accommodation and supporting facilities at hotels, resorts and guesthouses under *jasa perhotelan* for **PBJT**, the tax on certain goods and services. It includes hotel meeting room rental in this category and treats food and beverage as a separate category. A hotel that leases premises to a business to operate its own outlet should flag that agreement for review. Article 53 excludes letting premises to be operated as a business from the hotel-services category. [Indonesia’s Law No. 1 of 2022, Articles 50–53](https://www.peraturan.go.id/files/uu1-2022bt.pdf)

Check the rules in the property’s regency or city and confirm how they apply to the services you supply. For each component of a package or banquet, keep the invoice line or package breakdown, the supplying legal entity, the amount and any separately tracked tax or service charge. Keep later credits and the reason for them with the original invoice. Give the preparer the contract and invoice together so they can check the package as sold. [Badung Regulation 7/2023](https://jdih.badungkab.go.id/produk-hukum/peraturan-perundang-undangan/peraturan-daerah/peraturan-daerah-kabupaten-badung-nomor-7-tahun-2023-tentang-pajak-daerah-dan-retribusi-daerah) · [Badung amendment 8/2025](https://jdih.badungkab.go.id/index.php/produk-hukum/peraturan-perundang-undangan/peraturan-daerah/peraturan-daerah-kabupaten-badung-nomor-8-tahun-2025-tentang-perubahan-atas-peraturan-daerah-nomor-7-tahun-2023-tentang-pajak-daerah-dan-retribusi-daerah) · [Gianyar Regulation 7/2023](https://jdih.gianyarkab.go.id/produk/produk-hukum/detail/VlI0T1dMZEVWYkhLZ3hucjVnU1pXdz09)

## Track group deposits, guest balances and company invoices

A retreat organiser may reserve ten rooms and book catering for one dinner. Sales needs the room block and the names or rooming list. Finance needs a signed agreement, the payer, the deposit conditions, and which items belong on the group’s master folio. Write down whether individual guests pay for incidentals. When front desk moves a room charge between folios, keep the original reference and check that it leaves one folio as it joins the other. The same rule applies if a guest checks out early and the organiser agrees to cover an extra meal.

Keep a deposit schedule by reservation or group. For each deposit, record receipt date, bank reference, amount received, applicable booking terms, and the amounts applied, refunded or still held. If the hotel receives IDR 5,000,000 in December for a January event, keep the collection attached to the January booking and review it under your accounting policy. When the hotel delivers the agreed services and applies that advance to the bill, update the deposit schedule and the folio together. This lets finance explain why the bank receipt and the event revenue arrived in different months. For cancellations or group reductions, keep the signed terms and the subsequent agreement, then have finance review the amount that is refunded, retained or transferred to a future date.

A guest folio can also be closed to a company or travel agent account for later payment. Keep the final customer invoice, folio references, approval for credit, payment terms and correspondence together. Once the amount is transferred, confirm it appears on the correct company receivable and has cleared the guest ledger. A hotel can show a successful checkout even though it still needs to collect the balance. An aged receivables list should identify each amount and its due date so the sales or accounts team knows which invoices require follow-up.

Use the guest ledger to check how the open balance moved overnight. On another business date, assume it opens at IDR 12,000,000 and charges add IDR 7,400,000. The hotel posts IDR 4,200,000 in payments, transfers IDR 1,000,000 to a company account and applies IDR 2,000,000 from advance deposits. The guest ledger should close at IDR 12,200,000. Trace each payment to its tender, match the company transfer to the receivables schedule and check that the applied advance reduced the deposit balance. If the closing total matches, review those other schedules anyway. A company invoice posted to the wrong payer can balance in total and still be difficult to collect. [Oracle’s hotel trial balance report guide](https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/hospitality/opera-cloud/26.2/ocsuh/c_reports_trial_balance_report_trial_balance.htm)

Look at the booking terms before deciding whether the guest, agent or platform owes the hotel. When a platform collects the guest payment, check the expected remittance or the virtual card amount against the reservation and folio. If the hotel collects, tie the guest payment to the actual tender and track any commission invoice according to the arrangement. Group settlements and adjustments can cover more than one guest. Keep a list of unsettled amounts by booking and counterparty and assign each one an expected settlement date. A payout discrepancy should stay on that list until an explanation and supporting record are available. [Booking.com’s payment and payout details](https://developers.booking.com/connectivity/docs/payments-api/managing-payment-and-payout-details)

## Check the month’s occupancy, room rate and contribution

A monthly hotel report can include three room performance measures. **Occupancy** divides room nights sold by available room nights. **Average daily rate**, or **ADR**, divides room revenue by room nights sold. **Revenue per available room**, or **RevPAR**, divides room revenue by available room nights. When preparing a benchmark submission, use the benchmark’s definitions of revenue, paid room nights and available supply. A room taken off sale for a brief repaint can reduce the inventory the front desk can sell while remaining in the inventory reported to STR. Keep a record of these differences and use comparable definitions for both periods when explaining a change. [CoStar’s STR reporting rules for inventory and revenue](https://www.costar.com/products/str-benchmark/resources/guidelines/historical-benchmarking-data-reporting-guidelines)

For a simple example, a hotel has 24 rooms available throughout a 30-day month, giving it 720 available room nights. It sells 540 room nights and earns IDR 675,000,000 in room revenue, excluding restaurant and spa sales. Occupancy is 75%. ADR is IDR 1,250,000 and RevPAR is IDR 937,500. Calculate the month from the underlying revenue and night totals. Averaging daily percentages and rates can hide the difference between busy and quiet days because the number of nights sold can vary each day.

Add a departmental view to explain what the hotel kept from those sales. Decide which costs can be traced directly to each department and apply any shared-cost allocation consistently. Use laundry invoices and work schedules to explain room costs. Compare restaurant sales with the food and drink used, and trace spa sales to therapist hours, contractor invoices and supplies. Label which expenses have been deducted so the owner knows what the subtotal covers. If a restaurant manager supplies a separate cost report, reconcile it to the same period as the sales figures before comparing the margins.

Suppose a discount raises occupancy for two months. Higher room sales could be welcome, but the financial result also depends on which rates sold, what you paid for those bookings, and whether you needed more housekeeping or laundry. Review room revenue alongside channel costs and the additional costs of serving the stays. Then check what the guests spent in other departments. A package promotion can have a different result from a room-only discount even when the headline occupancy increase is similar. Bring each number back to its source when deciding whether to run the promotion again.

## Give the month-end close named owners

Use the nightly records to build a monthly pack. Give front desk responsibility for the signed business-date reports, outstanding folios and unresolved posting errors. Have outlet managers supply sales summaries, void approvals, stock counts and records of transfers between outlets. Give the bookkeeper responsibility for the ledger, bank and outstanding balances. Ask the duty manager or owner to review changes that cross months and changes to the way systems classify sales. A small hotel may give several jobs to the same person and arrange a separate review for refunds or large adjustments.

Check the cutoff for the last business date of the month. For a guest staying from 30 December to 3 January, the room charges may fall into both months even though the guest settles at a single checkout. Use the folio detail to review which services belong to each period. Find outlet charges posted after the reporting cutoff and determine the business date they relate to. Save both source reports and the authorised adjustment if the ledger has already been closed. Then check the supplier side. A December linen delivery can be a December cost even if the bill arrives in January, depending on your accounting policy and supporting records.

Reconcile five balances before review. Tie cash and card collections to deposits and settlement reports. Tie open guest folios to the guest ledger. Tie company invoices and booking intermediaries to their own receivable or settlement schedules. Match unapplied deposits to bookings still open or under review. Compare local tax figures with the underlying charge codes, invoices and adjustments. Record separately which items are waiting for normal settlement and which need a decision. If an expected transfer is late, specify the counterparty, amount and person who will contact it.

Have the reviewer pick a busy business date and trace one room sale and one outlet charge from the source to the ledger. Choose at least one item that crossed into the next month, such as an advance that is still held or an invoice collected after cutoff. The reviewer should be able to follow what happened without asking the shift team to reconstruct it. Preserve the final pack with both the reports and the evidence for adjustments so that next month’s opening balances are easy to check.
