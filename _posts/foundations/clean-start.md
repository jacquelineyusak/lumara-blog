Organized business records let someone follow a transaction without relying on memory. A practical starting system needs a consistent folder structure, a way to connect documents to transactions, and a visible list of missing information.

## Collect before you reorganize

Start by gathering the records currently spread across email, messaging apps, downloads and paper folders. Make an inventory of where records come from: sales software, payment providers, bank accounts, cash records and supplier emails.

Keep the original records. If several people hold copies of the same file, decide which is the source copy before deleting or combining anything.

## Use a small, repeatable folder structure

Create a folder for each month, with sections for sales, purchases, bank and cash records, and questions to resolve. Keep company documents separately so recurring reference material is easy to find. Restrict payroll and other personal information to the people who need it.

Use a file name that communicates the date, counterparty and document reference. For example, `2026-11-02_supplier_invoice-1042.pdf` is easier to match than `scan-final-new.pdf`.

## Connect each document to a transaction

For each record, identify:

1. What was sold, purchased or received?
2. Which business activity does it relate to?
3. What are the date, amount and currency?
4. Has it been paid, partly paid or left outstanding?
5. Which transaction or ledger entry should it support?
6. Does another person need to clarify or review it?

Keep a document link or reference in the working transaction register. Where the meaning is unclear, record the question rather than guessing the category.

## Give every open question an owner

Use simple statuses such as “to review”, “ready to record” and “complete”. For incomplete items, add the missing information, the person responsible and the next follow-up date.

A weekly review keeps the list small. At month-end, confirm which accounts and document sources are included in the handover so that a missing account is not mistaken for a quiet month.

## Prepare a clear handover

The first handover to a bookkeeper should include the period covered, the account list, the available records and the known gaps. Agree how future files will be shared and who will answer questions from the business.
