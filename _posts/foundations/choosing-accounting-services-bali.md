Two accounting quotes can have the same monthly fee and leave your team with very different amounts of work. One provider might match platform settlements to your sales reports and bank deposits. Another might expect your staff to supply an already reconciled spreadsheet. Both can offer monthly bookkeeping. The difference becomes clear when a delivery app deposits less than your sales report shows and someone needs to find out why.

Start by describing the work your business needs to get done. For a café in Bali, that may mean knowing whether daily sales reached the right bank account and how much is due to suppliers next week. For a foreign-owned Indonesian company, often called a PT PMA, the local finance team might also need to give directors overseas an English-language report and explain how it ties to Indonesian records. You can compare providers more easily when each is pricing the same work and your team knows what it will still need to do.

## Ask for the output from each service

### Bookkeeping

Bookkeeping records transactions and checks those records against source documents. Ask which bank accounts, cash floats, payment gateways and sales systems the provider will reconcile. For sales paid by card, find out whether the work stops at matching a combined settlement to a bank credit or goes back to the individual sales, refunds and fees that make up the settlement. A useful close includes the reconciliations and details of any amounts still waiting for an explanation.

Ask how the provider counts transactions. A single bank deposit may represent dozens of card sales and several adjustments. A business with 100 bank lines can have considerably more work behind those lines if it needs transaction-level reconciliation. Share a sample month with sensitive details removed. Have the provider show which rows it would count toward the quote and what changes during a busy month.

### Financial reporting

Financial reporting turns the books into information people can use. Name the decisions you want the reports to support. A general manager who checks margins by location needs a different view from a director who reviews the company’s overall cash position. Ask which statements and schedules are included, what detail each shows, and whether someone will walk you through unusual movements. If the report is meant for a lender, investor or parent company, share their requested format and timetable before you agree to the scope.

An English-language management pack can be useful for a director overseas. Agree who will reconcile it to the local books and how adjustments or currency conversions will be shown. If you need year-end financial statements for a particular purpose, specify them separately. Confirm the preparation, review and delivery responsibilities. A monthly management pack and an assurance engagement have different deliverables and should have their own written terms.

### Tax reporting and advice

Ask the provider to name the returns, entities and periods it will cover. For each return, agree who supplies the data, prepares the computation, reviews it, arranges payment, signs, submits and saves the filing evidence. Ask what happens when the company starts making a different type of payment or receives a request for information. It helps to know whether a question will be handled by the monthly team, passed to a specialist or quoted separately before work starts.

Monthly calculation and submission, annual return preparation, technical advice on a new transaction and representation on a tax matter are distinct assignments. A provider may handle some of them and coordinate with someone else on the rest. If two teams are involved, agree who owns the handoff. A tax preparer might need invoice details from one team, a classification decision from another and approval from your director before the return can move forward.

### Payroll and company compliance

Payroll can include preparing the monthly pay calculation, reporting employee tax, updating joiners and leavers, and handling tasks related to BPJS, Indonesia’s social security programmes. Ask which tasks are included and who approves the roster, bank details and final amounts. If your business hires seasonally, confirm how the scope scales and which employee changes your team needs to report before the payroll cutoff.

Company compliance can cover tracking renewal dates, collecting documents or preparing specific reports such as an investment activity report, known as LKPM, where applicable. Ask whether the provider monitors a date, prepares the supporting information or also handles submission under the agreed arrangement. Confirm who supplies the project information and who reviews it before anything is submitted. A calendar entry alone gives you a reminder. A complete scope tells you how the work gets done and where to find the submission record. [Lumara’s guide to preparing for LKPM reporting](/blog/compliance/lkpm-notes)

## Give each provider the same scope worksheet

Use a recent ordinary month and a busy month to describe your business. List the legal entities and locations, payment sources, approximate transaction counts, employee headcount, reports needed and known gaps in the records. Mention a planned opening, new sales channel or change in headcount if it would affect the first few months. At the quotation stage, totals and a redacted example are usually enough to explain the workload.

Send each provider the same worksheet. Ask them to fill in the service they propose, their assumptions and any fee for the work. Give each row an owner on your side wherever an input or approval is needed. Once the offers come back, review any row left blank before you compare totals.

| Work to compare | Describe your business | Ask the provider to specify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Books and reconciliation | Entities, bank accounts, cash floats, sales systems, settlement sources, normal and peak volumes | Sources covered, transaction counting method, checks performed and open-item report |
| Monthly close and reports | Reporting date, users, languages, currencies and location or project detail | Delivery date, statements, schedules, review call and revisions |
| Recurring tax work | Registrations, currently filed returns, supporting records, person who approves payment | Returns and periods covered, preparation, review, signing, submission and receipts |
| Payroll | Headcount, pay cycle, typical staff changes and current payroll process | Inputs, cutoffs, calculations, employee tax and BPJS tasks, file delivery |
| Company compliance | Relevant company records, reporting locations and upcoming obligations | Monitoring, preparation and submission responsibilities, evidence delivered |
| Irregular work | Backlog, opening balances, year-end requirements and upcoming changes | One-off fees, extra work rates, approval threshold and proposed end date |

Set a deadline for your team to supply complete files. Ask the provider when they will flag missing records and when you will receive the draft and final outputs. If you need the monthly report before a meeting with partners, put the meeting date on the worksheet. Work back from it and check whether everyone has enough time to review figures and answer questions. If the date slips, agree how the provider distinguishes a late close from a provisional report that needs more work.

## Compare two offers against the same job

### A café needs payment reconciliation across three channels

Consider a café with counter sales, takeaway orders from a delivery platform and deposits for group bookings. Its manager already closes the till daily. The owner wants each month’s report to explain card fees, delivery-platform deductions and deposits that will be applied to later events. The café also needs a named tax preparer for the agreed returns. There are three months of earlier settlement differences to clear up before the opening balances are reliable.

In a hypothetical comparison, Provider A quotes IDR 3.1 million per month for covering the till, bank and group deposit schedule. It adds IDR 900,000 to reconcile delivery settlements and IDR 1.2 million for the agreed tax work. Provider B quotes IDR 4.9 million per month for all three groups of work, including the deposit schedule. On the café’s required scope, A is IDR 5.2 million per month and B is IDR 4.9 million. The owner can now compare their inputs, deadlines and checks using the same job. These figures illustrate the comparison and are made up for this example.

The opening cleanup still needs its own price and an end point. If A quotes IDR 1.5 million for the backlog and B quotes IDR 2.7 million, the first three months at the quoted scope come to IDR 17.1 million with A and IDR 17.4 million with B. From month four, B is IDR 300,000 less per month at the same volume. Before deciding, the owner should confirm that both providers define the three months of cleanup the same way, use the same assumptions about seasonal volume, and specify any applicable taxes or software costs on their fees. The numbers only help when the underlying work is comparable.

### A consultancy can keep bookkeeping in-house

A PT PMA consultancy has a finance assistant who can reconcile its bank accounts and prepare the monthly transaction detail. An overseas director reviews its cash forecast. Its main needs are a second review before the books close and help with agreed tax reporting. From time to time it receives an invoice from a related company abroad and needs to know who can review the supporting documents and advise on the treatment.

One quote includes full monthly bookkeeping and standard management statements. The other starts with the assistant’s reconciled ledger, reviews selected schedules, prepares named returns and quotes separately for technical advice. The consultancy can ask for a price on the second scope from both firms. It should agree who checks the output against the local ledger, who raises unusual payments for review and how quickly someone can respond before a payment is approved.

There is still an operating choice to make. If the assistant goes on leave or takes on another role, the review-only service may need someone else to prepare the records. Ask how much notice the provider needs to take on that temporary work and how it is priced. Conversely, if the company keeps the assistant’s role, agree which tasks a full service would take over and give the assistant a clear responsibility for inputs and approvals. Test the arrangement on a representative month before extending it across further entities.

### Count the time your team will spend

A quote can list the same deliverables as another and still depend on more preparation by your staff. Ask each provider to describe what a complete set of inputs looks like. The consultancy’s assistant may be able to export a ledger in minutes. If the provider also needs an invoice register in a particular format, a reconciliation by currency and a separate schedule of charges from the parent company, producing the input will take longer. Put that time on the worksheet and name who will do the work when the assistant is away.

Check how the arrangement will hold up in your busiest month. The café may process twice as many delivery orders during a holiday period. If the provider charges for extra volume, ask for the calculation at that level. If your own team is expected to review delivery adjustments before handing them over, work out when someone on shift can do it. This gives you a better basis for comparing the fees and deciding which jobs to keep in-house.

## Check who will do the work and who can act for you

Ask for the names or roles of the person who prepares the records, the reviewer and the backup when someone is away. Ask for a blank or anonymised report and example of how they communicate an exception. You might give them a sample discrepancy between a booking report and a bank deposit and ask what evidence they would request. Their answer can show how they investigate a real question and when they involve your team.

If you can speak to a reference, ask about an assignment similar to yours and get permission before requesting an introduction. A business that needs monthly reconciliations across several sales channels should ask how the provider handled missing reports and whether its team could explain a large difference. Where the provider keeps client work confidential, an anonymised walkthrough can still show who investigated the discrepancy, when they escalated it and what record they returned.

If you intend to appoint a proxy to act for the company on specific Indonesian tax matters, ask for the individual’s name, the basis on which they can act, and the exact activities and periods covered. The rules require a special power of attorney for the specified tax matters and set eligibility requirements. If you engage a tax consultant for a foreign-investment company such as a PT PMA, ask to see their class C licence. Check the named consultant’s status in the Ministry of Finance’s public lookup using their name or licence decision number. Keep a record of whom you appointed and what the appointment covers. [Ministry of Finance rules on tax proxies, Articles 7–8](https://jdih.kemenkeu.go.id/dok/pmk-44-tahun-2026) · [Tax consultant licensing rules, Articles 3–4](https://www.pajak.go.id/id/peraturan/konsultan-pajak-dan-pihak-lain-yang-bertindak-sebagai-kuasa-wajib-pajak) · [Ministry of Finance tax consultant lookup](https://find-profkeu.kemenkeu.go.id/Kp/PeriksaKp)

Agree the system access separately from the scope of any appointment. Indonesia’s tax authority, DJP, provides instructions for granting and removing drafter and signer roles in Coretax. Confirm which person needs each role and who in the company will manage access. For bookkeeping, ask whether the provider can work from a read-only feed or secure exports. Keep payment approval with named people in your business, and agree how to withdraw access and retrieve your records when the engagement ends. [DJP guide to assigning and revoking Coretax roles](https://www.pajak.go.id/coretaxpedia/beri-cabut-role)

## Specify what a good first month looks like

Put the terms into the engagement letter or statement of work. Include the start date, delivery dates, files to be returned, assumptions about volume, fees for extra work, and who approves changes before a charge is incurred. If the business has a backlog, name the periods and define how the clean-up will be accepted. Agree how you will get a copy of the underlying data if you change providers or bring work in-house. Use the first reporting cycle to check that the written scope works with the business’s actual records. Pick a bank settlement and ask to see how it ties back to the sales, refunds and fees included in the batch. Find an unpaid supplier invoice and check that its balance and due date match the supporting document. For an agreed tax filing, ask for the reviewed computation and proof of submission once available. If the report has unresolved items, confirm their value, who will investigate them and when you will review the result.

Before the next month begins, go back to the decisions that made you seek help. Check whether you can use the reports to plan payments, discuss margins with your managers or answer questions from directors. Where the information is missing, trace the gap to the records, the scope or the delivery timetable. Then make a written change that your team and the provider can follow on every subsequent close.
