Two guests check out of a Bali villa on the last day of the month. One paid through a booking platform, which sends the proceeds a few days later. The other booked directly and paid a deposit weeks earlier. The bank statement shows cash from both guests, but at different points in the booking cycle. If you use the bank feeds alone to prepare the accounts, you can put a stay in the wrong month, lose sight of a fee, or pay out money that should still be available for a refund.

For a single-villa business, good bookkeeping explains what the villa earned and how much cash is available for operating bills. For multiple villas, it also shows which properties are carrying their costs. A management company has another job. It needs to account for what it earned under each agreement and explain what happened to the money it handled for each owner. The same reconciliation can supply much of the information for all three situations.

## Give every reservation an accounting record

Start with a reservation register that covers platform and direct bookings. It can be a spreadsheet, an export from a property management system, or a report produced from both. Give each booking a stable reference and attach the channel, property, arrival and departure dates, and booking status. For direct bookings, use a reference that can also appear on the guest invoice and payment instructions. If a guest moves between two units, preserve the original reference and show how you allocated the amounts between properties.

Keep the dates of the reservation, the stay, the guest payment, and the bank settlement in separate fields. The booking date helps you track commitments. The stay dates tell you when the accommodation was provided. The payment and settlement dates tell you where the cash is. For an accrual-based close, review the nights delivered on either side of month-end and apply your accounting policy to the revenue and related costs. A five-night stay with different weekday and weekend prices should use the documented nightly charges where available. Keep the allocation with the month-end workings so it can be reproduced.

Track at least two types of advance cash separately. A booking advance is applied to a later bill under the booking terms. A refundable damage deposit is money held subject to return or an approved deduction. Keep a list showing each open amount, the guest reference, and when the balance is due to be applied or returned. When part of a damage deposit is retained, document the reason and amount and record the outcome under your accounting policy. That trail makes it easier to respond to a guest who asks for the calculation weeks later.

An export that only shows confirmed bookings can miss later changes. Save a regular record of cancellations, no-shows, shortened stays, discounts and refunds too. Preserve the original booking reference when a later adjustment is made, and add the date and reason for the change. Ask the person who approved a refund to attach the platform message, payment reference or other supporting record while it is still easy to find.

## Match bookings to settlements, then settlements to the bank

A reservation report and a bank statement serve different purposes. Use a payout or processor report between them. Start with the amounts attributable to the operation for each booking as shown in the reservation records. Identify discounts, operator-borne refunds, platform fees and any tax amounts shown separately. A platform may charge the guest a separate fee, so check which amount belongs to the business before building your reconciliation. Airbnb’s earnings reports, for example, show gross earnings, adjustments, host service fees, taxes withheld and net pay, and can be filtered by listing and payout method. [Airbnb’s guide to earnings reports](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3632)

Use the payout reference to group the items that belong to one transfer. One bank credit can cover more than one reservation or listing. Retain the breakdown by booking even when you match the bank entry as a batch. If a payout includes a deduction from an earlier stay, record the reference for that stay on the adjustment row. Airbnb describes how certain changes and refunds can create adjustments against a later payout. If the earlier reservation is already closed, send that item to whoever reviews prior-period changes and retain the link between both records. [Airbnb’s payout tracking guide](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/304) · [Airbnb’s guide to earnings adjustments](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/366)

Confirm how each channel collects and pays. Booking.com, for example, describes bank-transfer configurations that deduct commission and payment fees from the payout and configurations that settle gross, with commission paid separately. It also distinguishes bookings collected through its payments service from bookings paid at the property. Check the settlement setting and the individual reservation before deciding which bank receipt or commission invoice to match. [Booking.com’s guide to payouts](https://developers.booking.com/connectivity/docs/payments-by-booking-onboarding-api/understanding-payouts) · [Booking.com’s guide to payment and payout details](https://developers.booking.com/connectivity/docs/payments-api/managing-payment-and-payout-details)

For direct bookings, apply the same principle when a card processor settles several payments together. Match the guest invoice or deposit request to the processor transaction, then trace the processor batch to the bank. For a guest who pays by bank transfer, match the reference and amount directly. Where a payout and your bank account use different currencies, preserve the original amounts and the IDR amount the bank credited. Identify an exchange difference or bank charge using the available settlement evidence so it can be recorded separately from a booking adjustment.

Keep a running list of items that still need a match. For each item, note the booking or payout reference, expected amount, due date and person following up. Plausible timing differences include a payout initiated near month-end or a card settlement due after a holiday. An unexplained difference should stay visible until you can trace it to a fee, adjustment, exchange difference, failed payment or missing record. If you use a clearing account to track payouts in transit, compare its balance to the open items and carry the unresolved details into the next close.

### A worked example in rupiah

Assume the villa operator supplies the accommodation. In this illustration it has an IDR 8,000,000 room charge and an IDR 400,000 cleaning charge after the stay. The operator agrees to an IDR 600,000 partial refund on the room. The platform deducts an IDR 400,000 host fee and sends the rest in one settlement. The example excludes tax, guest-paid platform fees and currency conversion so that you can follow the money through the records.

| Item from the booking or payout report | IDR |
| --- | ---: |
| Accommodation charge | 8,000,000 |
| Cleaning charge | 400,000 |
| Room refund funded by the operator | (600,000) |
| Charges after the refund | 7,800,000 |
| Host fee deducted by the platform | (400,000) |
| Bank payout | 7,400,000 |

An operator who enters the IDR 7,400,000 bank deposit in a single sales line loses the fee and refund breakdown. With the underlying data, you can explain the IDR 7,800,000 in charges after the refund and see the IDR 400,000 cost of the channel. The cleaning charge in the table is billed to the guest. The amount paid to the cleaning provider comes from a separate supplier invoice or payroll record and has its own date and property reference. If you want to compare whether direct and platform bookings make similar contributions, you need both the actual cost of delivering the stay and the cost of collecting it.

## Make owner money and management fees visible

A management agreement can give an operator authority to collect guest payments, approve small repairs and remit the balance to the owner. Other agreements have the owner collect payments directly while the manager issues a monthly service invoice. A company might also operate one property on its own account and handle receipts on behalf of the owner at another. For each property, read the signed agreement alongside the actual guest booking terms. Identify who supplies the accommodation, who carries the relevant obligations, who invoices the guest, who receives the cash, and how the manager earns its fee. Where principal-versus-agent accounting applies, the role depends on control of the promised service. Receipt into a particular bank account by itself provides limited evidence about that role. Review the applicable reporting framework and the real arrangement with your accountant. [IFRS Foundation’s principal-versus-agent guidance](https://www.ifrs.org/news-and-events/updates/ifric/2022/ifric-update-april-2022/)

In a separate scenario, assume the owner supplies the accommodation and a manager receives the same IDR 7,400,000 payout on the owner’s behalf. Start with an opening owner balance of zero. The signed agreement allows the manager to apply IDR 1,200,000 for a separately earned management fee and IDR 500,000 for an approved repair paid on the owner’s behalf. The activity to explain on the owner statement is the IDR 7,400,000 received, the two supported deductions, and the IDR 5,700,000 available for remittance. After transferring IDR 5,700,000, the remaining amount payable for those transactions is zero. If the manager has already invoiced its fee, match the deduction against that invoice to avoid recording the same income twice.

Before issuing owner statements, sum each owner’s activity from the opening balance through receipts, fees, approved expenses and remittances. Tie the resulting balances back to your books. Some owners leave money with the manager for future maintenance. Track how much each owner has authorised the manager to hold and what the agreement permits it to cover. If the bookkeeper posted a payment for Villa A against Villa B’s owner balance, correct both property records and retain the explanation for both statements. A mismatch of this kind can leave the total cash balance unchanged while giving two owners the wrong answers.

Give owners a short explanation when cash and performance differ materially. An owner might see less cash this month because an adjustment for an earlier booking reduced a payout. The operating statement can show the current month’s stays and costs under the agreed reporting basis, while the owner funds statement explains the cash movement and remaining balance. Include the bank transfer reference with each remittance. If the owner receives platform transfers directly, obtain the agreed access to settlement reports so the owner can reconcile the records against their own account.

## Allocate the costs you need to run the villas

Tag a cost to the property that incurred it wherever the source record allows. Laundry, guest supplies, utilities and minor repairs commonly have a location, service period and supplier on the invoice. A shared security contract or a staff member who covers several villas may need a consistent allocation. Choose a basis that reflects the cost. A staff rota can support a wage allocation by hours worked. A shared booking system fee might follow the number of listings covered. Record the basis once and retain the data used each month. If the arrangement changes, explain the change so a margin comparison remains meaningful.

Separate recurring costs from significant replacements and improvements for review. A late invoice for routine pool maintenance belongs in the month-end cutoff discussion. A major pool overhaul raises different questions about what the project did, which entity paid and whether the cost should be capitalised under the applicable accounting policy. Give your accountant the scope, supplier invoices, completion evidence and contractual responsibility. If shareholders or owners paid directly, keep proof and identify whether the business owes a reimbursement or whether a documented funding arrangement applies.

Select a small set of measures that help you make decisions. Compare room revenue and channel fees by booking source. Review direct operating cost per paid night for similar properties, and explain how you treat owner stays, maintenance closures and complimentary nights. Those nights may reduce the inventory available to sell or generate costs of their own. Use the same definitions when comparing months. A villa that appears more profitable after a brief closure may simply have shifted maintenance invoices into the following month.

## Collect the information behind local tax figures

Indonesia’s framework for **PBJT**, the tax on certain goods and services, includes villa accommodation within the hospitality category. Local rules determine how the framework applies in the relevant regency or city. Check which entity supplies the stay, the property’s location, the nature of each charge and who is responsible for local reporting. Review management fees and other separately supplied services according to their own facts and applicable rules. [Indonesia’s Regional Financial Relations Law, Articles 50, 53 and 57–59](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Download/189520/UU%20Nomor%201%20Tahun%202022.pdf)

Set up your reconciliation to preserve enough detail for that review. Save guest invoices, booking reports, fee invoices, refund approvals and evidence of any tax shown as collected, withheld or remitted. Record the property and entity on each file. When a platform report shows a tax line, identify the tax and what the line represents before treating it as settled. For several villas across different jurisdictions, keep the amounts attributable to each property visible in the reporting file. A useful handoff shows your tax preparer how to move from transactions to totals, including the treatment of cancellations and month-end timing.

## Use the same close each month

At the start of the close, collect the reservation exports, platform payouts, bank statements, processor reports, supplier invoices and owner agreements or amendments. Set a cutoff for property managers to submit missing invoices and explain adjustments. A single villa can use the same process as a portfolio, with fewer files. Give each document a consistent name and have one person confirm that each channel and bank account is represented before reconciliation begins.

Match reservations to settlements and bank receipts. Review open advances, damage deposits, refunds, outstanding payouts and amounts due to owners. Then check whether the month’s supplier costs are complete and whether any expenses were recorded against the wrong property. Keep a short list of unresolved items with their value and who will respond. Where the amounts could affect an owner remittance or a tax report, settle the classification or obtain a review before using the figures for that decision.

Finish by producing the property results and any owner statements due. Have a second person review owner bank details against the approved record before sending funds. Check that the closing owner balances and the list of unsettled payouts agree with the books, then carry forward the underlying detail. When you can follow a booking from the guest record all the way to the bank and the owner statement, the figures can answer practical questions about a disputed refund, a missing transfer or a period of unusually high costs.
