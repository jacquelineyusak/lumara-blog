An orderly switch starts with an agreed cut-off date and a clear record of what the outgoing provider has completed. Before a new provider begins, identify the last reconciled period, the work still outstanding and the person responsible for each upcoming obligation.

This is a practical handover checklist. The exact documents depend on the company, existing agreement and services being transferred.

## Agree the cut-off and responsibilities

Write down the final period covered by the outgoing provider and the first period covered by the new provider. Distinguish the date records are handed over from the dates a report is prepared, approved or submitted.

List work in progress separately. For each open task, record its status, the next action and who will complete it. This helps avoid an obligation falling between two providers who both believe the other is handling it.

## Request a usable records pack

| Area | Records to request or locate |
| --- | --- |
| Bookkeeping | Ledger exports, chart of accounts, trial balance and the last completed period’s working files |
| Reconciliation | Bank and cash reconciliation records with explanations for outstanding differences |
| Sales and suppliers | Unpaid invoice lists and supporting documents for outstanding balances |
| Other working balances | Relevant schedules and explanations for balances carried into the new period |
| Tax work | Copies of completed filings, payment or submission evidence and unresolved correspondence |
| Company administration | The company documents and compliance records relevant to the agreed new scope |
| Open issues | Missing documents, unfinished corrections and questions requiring a business decision |

Ask for an export format the new provider can actually use. A folder of final reports may not include the transaction-level records needed to continue the books.

## Check the opening position

Have the incoming provider identify which balances and periods can be relied on and which require clarification. Keep differences visible in a handover issues list. If cleanup is needed, agree its scope before treating it as part of routine monthly work.

For a simple example, an unpaid supplier balance should be supported by an invoice list and any relevant payments. If that detail is missing, record the gap rather than assuming the total is correct because it appears in a report.

## Transfer access carefully

Inventory the systems involved, the account owner and the level of access required. Use the systems’ supported access or authorization process and confirm the business retains appropriate control. Do not email passwords in a shared spreadsheet. Remove obsolete access when the handover is complete and responsibilities have been confirmed.

For Indonesian tax-system access, refer to the account-role guidance linked from the [official DJP Coretax resource page](https://www.pajak.go.id/id/reformdjp/coretax), and confirm the appropriate arrangement for the company.

## Review the first handover together

At the first review, confirm the account list, opening position, outstanding tasks and upcoming internal deadlines. Agree how documents will be shared, who answers questions and what the business receives after each close.
