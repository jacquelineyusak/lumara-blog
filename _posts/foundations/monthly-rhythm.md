A monthly bookkeeping close turns a month of transactions into records that can be checked and used. The practical goal is to know which accounts have been reconciled, which documents are missing, and which questions still need a decision.

For a small business, consistency matters more than an elaborate process. Choose a cut-off, agree who supplies the documents and use the same handover checklist each month.

## A five-step monthly close

1. **Collect the period’s records.** Gather sales documents, purchases, payment records and statements for every account used by the business. Confirm that statement periods are complete.
2. **Check completeness.** Compare the document list with the transaction records. Identify gaps, duplicates and transactions that belong to another period.
3. **Reconcile.** Match the ledger with bank and cash records, and investigate differences. Keep a note of unresolved items rather than hiding them in an unexplained adjustment.
4. **Review the open items.** Check unpaid customer invoices, supplier balances, unusual transactions and missing information. Give each follow-up an owner.
5. **Save the close pack.** Store the final working files, reconciliation notes and a short summary of what changed. Make clear which items are complete and which remain open.

## Use a one-page close summary

A simple summary can contain the period, accounts included, documents received, reconciliation status and open questions. Add the responsible person and next action for each exception.

For example, an entry such as “supplier invoice missing” should identify the payment date and amount, who will request the invoice and when the issue will be reviewed again. It then becomes a manageable task instead of a vague reminder.

## Keep bookkeeping and tax reporting connected

The bookkeeping close provides an organized starting point for tax work. It does not automatically mean the applicable tax returns have been prepared or submitted. Agree the handover date, the filing scope and the person responsible for each next step.

If an error is found after a close, keep a record of the correction and tell the person using the original figures. This helps avoid different versions circulating without explanation.

## Choose a scope the business can maintain

The routine should reflect transaction volume, number of accounts, the quality of incoming documents and the people involved. A provider can only work efficiently when the business supplies the agreed information and answers questions promptly.
