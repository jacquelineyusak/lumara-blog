At closing, the point-of-sale system shows a busy night. There is cash in the drawer, a batch total on the card terminal, several QRIS payments in the merchant app and delivery orders marked complete. The bank will tell you what arrived over the next few days. To explain how much the restaurant sold and what it cost to serve those orders, you also need the shift and supplier records.

A bookkeeping routine for a Bali restaurant starts with three questions. Can you match each sale to a payment or explain why the amount is still outstanding? Can you show how much food the kitchen used during the same period? Can you connect the shifts people worked to the wages paid or owed? The answers give a café owner a way to investigate a disappointing margin even in a month when sales look strong.

## Close the shift around a single sales record

Choose the source you use to count sales. For many restaurants that is a point-of-sale system, or POS, together with an export for orders the POS cannot capture. Give each location and selling channel a distinct code. Preserve completed orders, refunds, voids, discounts, payment methods and who approved an adjustment. If a delivery integration already sends orders into the POS, use the delivery export to match the orders and reconcile payment. Importing its sales again would count those orders twice.

Set one business-day cutoff and apply it consistently. A bar that stays open past midnight might assign the entire shift to one trading date in its operating report. Its terminal could settle after midnight, and its provider export may use a different cutoff. Record the trading date, payment timestamp and settlement date separately. If the POS and payout reports disagree about the date, search by transaction reference before assuming a sale is missing. At month-end, give your bookkeeper the cutoff rules for each system so they can review transactions close to the boundary.

Have the closing manager save the sales total and a breakdown by payment method. Run an exception report for unusual discounts, reopened orders, split payments, voids and refunds. A void made before payment, such as removing the wrong dish from an open table, calls for a different record from a refund on a closed bill. For a refund, link the original order to the approved reason and any payment-provider confirmation. If a guest splits the bill between cash and QRIS, keep the two payments attached to the same order. Otherwise, it can look as if two orders were paid or one payment was missed.

Capture price changes at the source. A restaurant promotion can lower the amount the restaurant earns, and the platform may fund part of a discount under the campaign terms. Save the order breakdown showing who paid for the promotion. For a complimentary dish, keep the reason and quantity so the kitchen can still account for ingredients used. For a booking advance or a gift voucher, track the money received and its later use or refund. Let the bookkeeper review the applicable treatment with the terms and the sales records in hand. ## Reconcile each payment method to its own evidence

For cash, start with the opening float. Add cash takings and subtract documented cash refunds and approved payments from the till. Count the drawer and compare the two totals. Have the manager record any overage or shortage separately, then record the amount transferred to the safe or prepared for deposit and the float left for the next shift. If ingredients are purchased out of the till, attach the receipt and log the payee. Reconcile the safe and bank deposit separately when the cash actually moves.

For cards and QRIS, match each payment to the provider’s merchant record. QRIS is Indonesia’s standard for QR payments. Bank Indonesia recommends confirming payment in the merchant’s own account and checking its transaction history if the notification is missing. Keep the payment reference so it can be matched to the sale and the later settlement. Any applicable QRIS merchant discount rate, or MDR, is borne by the merchant. Use the provider’s actual statement for the fees and expected settlement. When the bank credits one batch for several payments, keep the transaction detail behind that batch. [Bank Indonesia’s QRIS payment confirmation guide](https://www.bi.go.id/id/publikasi/ruang-media/cerita-bi/Pages/cara-memastikan-pembayaran-qris-benar-benar-masuk.aspx) · [Bank Indonesia’s guide to QRIS merchant fees](https://www.bi.go.id/id/publikasi/ruang-media/cerita-bi/Pages/mdr-qris.aspx)

For delivery, start by matching the platform order reference to the POS or supplemental sales register. Then use the provider’s transaction and payout reports to reconcile the amount the restaurant was entitled to receive, deductions and bank transfer. Keep the statement detail behind commissions, marketing charges, refunds and credits. GrabMerchant, for example, provides transaction exports, information about transfers and refunds, and downloadable reports and invoices. Its report definitions include a settlement ID and transfer date. Confirm which dates and outlets the export covers and whether the provider’s order total includes taxes or other charges. [GrabMerchant’s guide to its finance reports](https://merchant.grab.com/id-id/guides/default/kemudahan-kelola-bisnis-dengan-grabmerchant-portal) · [GrabMerchant’s explanation of report fields](https://hodor-assets.grab.com/attachments/merchant/id-id/articles/28623/rc-upload-1763015397424-2-GMR%20Definition%20-%20Definisi%20Laporan%20GrabMerchant.pdf?grab_external=true)

Keep electronic receipts that are still due in a separate reconciliation schedule, often supported by a clearing account in the ledger. Each open row should have the payment channel, order or batch reference, amount, expected settlement and the person following up. A balance can make sense when a provider batches evening transactions for the next processing day. An overdue balance requires a check for a failed or disputed payment, a fee amendment, a deposit to another account or a missing export. Carry the open rows into the next period and clear them against the matching bank entry. That prevents a late-arriving deposit from looking like sales for the new month.

### A shift reconciliation in rupiah

Suppose the menu value of completed orders is IDR 5,600,000 and the restaurant funded IDR 200,000 of discounts. The sales total for this example is IDR 5,400,000, with tax, service charges and tips left out for clarity. The tender report allocates the balance to cash, QRIS, card and platform-collected delivery. The provider statements show the following fees and expected payouts. The amounts are illustrative and the actual charges come from each provider’s statement and agreement.

| Tender | Sales allocated | Settlement deductions | Expected electronic payout |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Cash | IDR 1,400,000 | — | — |
| QRIS | IDR 1,800,000 | IDR 8,000 | IDR 1,792,000 |
| Card | IDR 1,000,000 | IDR 15,000 | IDR 985,000 |
| Delivery platform | IDR 1,200,000 | IDR 260,000 | IDR 940,000 |
| Total | IDR 5,400,000 | IDR 283,000 | IDR 3,717,000 |

The team can trace IDR 4,000,000 in electronic payments to IDR 3,717,000 in expected bank credits and IDR 283,000 in deductions. The delivery statement shows that its IDR 260,000 deduction includes IDR 240,000 in commission and IDR 20,000 in advertising. Recording those separately helps the owner see how much was spent promoting the channel. When the three payouts clear, match them with their own batch references. If the IDR 940,000 arrives in a larger transfer, use the provider’s batch detail to confirm that the amount is included.

The till began with a float of IDR 500,000. The IDR 1,400,000 in cash takings and a documented IDR 100,000 produce purchase give an expected drawer total of IDR 1,800,000. A count of IDR 1,790,000 leaves a shortage of IDR 10,000. The manager records it for follow-up, seals IDR 1,290,000 for deposit and signs off on the remaining IDR 500,000 float. Retain the cash count and note who makes the deposit. A second manager reviewing it tomorrow can trace the sale, purchase, variance and deposit separately.

## Use the stock count to explain ingredient cost

Supplier payments tell you when the business used its cash. To understand ingredient cost for a month, start with the stock on hand at its opening, add purchases received, and account for transfers, returns and stock physically on hand at the end. Match supplier invoices to goods received and use the same stock units and costing method each month. If your invoices are in cases, your recipes in grams and your count in kilograms, make the conversion explicit. For partly used or prepared stock, agree a measurement method the team can reproduce.

Schedule the count for a time when stock movement is low. A count after Sunday service is easier to use if any ingredients received or moved during the count are marked separately. Split the list by store room, prep station and bar. Ask a second person to recheck high-value items or quantities that move sharply from the last count. Keep supplier invoices and delivery notes for items received before month-end and invoiced later. Send the bookkeeper the receiving dates and agreed amounts so they can review the period in which purchases and outstanding bills belong.

Record central kitchen transfers with the sending and receiving outlets and a common transfer reference. For transfers between locations in the same company, use the record to move the ingredient cost to the location that received the stock and cancel the internal movement when reviewing the company total. A central kitchen supplying a different legal entity needs a separate review of how to document and account for those transactions. For both arrangements, give the receiving outlet a way to confirm what arrived and record missed or damaged items. Otherwise a dispatch can leave one kitchen's stock figures before it appears in the other’s.

Compare the ingredient cost from stock movements with what sold at standard recipe quantities and yields. Say a café starts the month with IDR 8,000,000 in food stock, receives IDR 22,000,000 of ingredients after returns and counts IDR 7,000,000 in closing stock. With no transfers, it used IDR 23,000,000 of food stock. Against IDR 72,000,000 in net food sales, that is approximately 31.9%. Recipes applied to the month’s sales suggest IDR 20,800,000, or 28.9%. To isolate differences in ingredient usage, use the same costing basis for both numbers. Review changes in supplier prices separately when you investigate the IDR 2,200,000 difference.

The IDR 23,000,000 measures everything that left stock, including ingredients used for spoiled batches and staff meals. If the kitchen log explains IDR 900,000 of waste and IDR 700,000 of authorised meals outside the recipe calculation, that leaves IDR 600,000 to investigate. Compare weights and portion sizes, check that all transfers were recorded and look for errors in the count or standard yield. Each business can present staff meals and unusual write-offs under its accounting policy. Keep the stock reconciliation intact so that any move to a separate ledger account leaves the total amount used traceable.

## Match labour cost to the shifts that generated it

Start with approved hours and rates for employees who work variable shifts. Include salaried staff when comparing the cost of running different outlets, and choose a consistent way to allocate someone who splits time between them. Compare the rota with approved timesheets, then match timesheets and agreed payroll changes to the payroll register. Document overtime approvals, advances, deductions and changes to start or end dates. The pay transfer should agree with the amount due to staff after deductions. Keep the gross wage cost and employer costs visible in the monthly report so a change in take-home pay does not obscure what it cost to staff the business.

Use the same period when comparing staffing and sales. A team could work the final weekend of a month and receive pay the following week. Ask the bookkeeper how the outstanding wages should be recorded at close. If a kitchen suddenly needs more paid hours, break the change down by shifts, opening hours and staffing levels. Added prep hours for a catering order and added cover for an absent employee call for different decisions. Where the venue collects service charges or tips for later distribution, keep them visible separately from wages, menu sales and petty cash, and confirm the applicable treatment with the payroll and accounting teams.

## Prepare local tax records outlet by outlet

Indonesia’s regional tax framework includes qualifying food and drink under PBJT, the tax on certain goods and services. Article 51 of Law No. 1 of 2022 describes covered restaurant and catering supplies and provides an exclusion tied to a turnover limit under the applicable local regulation. Identify the regency or city for each outlet, and confirm how its rules apply to the entity and its activities. A café that retails packaged products or caters across regency or city boundaries should review those revenue lines explicitly. Badung, for example, published its own local tax regulation and an amendment. [Indonesia’s regional financial relations law](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Download/189520/UU%20Nomor%201%20Tahun%202022.pdf) · [Badung’s local tax regulation](https://jdih.badungkab.go.id/index.php/produk-hukum/peraturan-perundang-undangan/peraturan-daerah/peraturan-daerah-kabupaten-badung-nomor-7-tahun-2023-tentang-pajak-daerah-dan-retribusi-daerah) · [Badung’s amendment](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/344913/perda-kab-badung-no-8-tahun-2025)

Keep the menu charge, discounts, service charges and any tax shown to the customer in separate fields on your sales report. Preserve the original receipt and order detail, including records for refunds, platform-funded promotions and adjustments. Tell your tax preparer if two outlets use different reporting setups or if one provider remits a tax amount under a specific arrangement. Ask what the statement documents and reconcile the reportable amounts to the underlying outlets and orders. Share any filing or payment evidence with the team that maintains the ledger so it can match the returns and payments to the right reporting period. ## What to review at the end of each month

Bring five records to the review. Use the daily sales summary, the till and safe reconciliation, the provider settlement schedule, the inventory calculation and the approved payroll summary. Check for missing shifts and payment batches, resolve differences that would affect the margin, and name the person working on each open item. Review food cost alongside discount usage and sales by channel. A delivery promotion can grow order volume and still reduce contribution once you include the ingredients, packaging, discount and commission the restaurant paid.

Start with one recent trading week when you set up the process. Have the manager close each shift and send the files while the payments and orders are still easy to trace. At the end of the week, choose one cash deposit, one QRIS batch and one delivery payout and walk each of them back to its orders. If you add another outlet, make sure its identifiers follow the same files through to the bank and keep separate results for each location.

If you want help organising the monthly books, describe your locations, POS setup, payment providers and stock records.
